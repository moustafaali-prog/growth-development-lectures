2Learning Objectives:

Learning Objectives:

2

Mastery of content in this chapter will enable the student to:

Describe normal growth and development and common parental concerns of the preschool period.

Assess a preschooler for normal growth and developmental milestones.

Formulate nursing diagnoses related to preschool growth and development and common parental concerns.

Identify appropriate outcomes for nursing care of

the preschooler.

3Slide 3

Plan nursing care to meet the preschooler's growth and

developmental needs, such as planning age-appropriate play activities.

Use critical thinking to analyze additional ways in which growth and developmental problems of the preschool child can be prevented and care can be family centered.

Integrate knowledge of preschool growth and development with nursing process to achieve quality child health nursing care.

3

4Preschool stage (3 -5 years)

Preschool stage (3 -5 years)

4

Child begins to separate from family

Gradually physical growth slows

Control of the body & coordination increases

Their world gets larger as they meet relatives, Friends, and neighbors.

5Physical Growth

Physical Growth

5

Taller & thinner because children tend to grow more in height than weight.

Brain reaches almost its adult size by 5 yrs.

The extremities of the body grow more quickly than the body trunk.

The posture of child gradually changes as the pelvis is straightened & the abdominal muscles become stronger.

6Slide 6

Weight:

6

Weight gain is generally slow, increasing between 18-20 kg About 3 to 5 kg. Yearly.

7Physiological Growth:

Physiological Growth:

7

Pulse: 80-120 beat / min

(average 100 beat / min).

Respiration: 20-30 cycle / min.

Blood pressure: 100/67 ±

24/25.

8Slide 8

Vision

Preschool are generally hyperopic (farsighted) unable to focus on near objects

Myopic (nearsighted).

Both situations need eye glasses.

This situation considered normal up to 5 years

9Slide 9

Hearing and taste:

The preschool has reached optimal level in ability to listen.

In relation to the sense of taste, he/she shows their preference by asking for something and may refuse something.

E . Discipline

20Psychosocial Development

Psychosocial Development

20

Egocentric in his thought & behavior, unable to see others viewpoints.

Tolerates separation from parents by 3 years

Separates easily by 5 years of age

Less dependent on parents but needs their reassurance & help

21Slide 21

According to Eriksson (initiative versus guilt)

Eriksson views crises at this time as important for the development of

the individual‘s self- concept & a sense of initiative

He/she wants to learn what to do for himself, learn about the world and other people.

If he fails to achieve this sense of initiative, he/she will develop a sense of guilt, where he will appear anxious and frightened in his contact with others

21

22Slide 22

Demonstrates strong attachment for parent of opposite sex (Oedipus & Electra complex)

More cooperative in play

Becomes interested in clothes & hair styles.

Freud theorizes that the preschooler is in the phallic stage of development

22

Emotional Development:

23

Fears the dark.

Tends to be impatient and selfish

Expresses aggression through physical & verbal behaviors.

Shows signs of jealousy.

Selected health problems

Fears

Common Fears:

Darkness

Being left alone

Animals

Body mutilation

Pain

30Slide 30

Sex Education

They are acutely aware of their sexuality, including sexual roles and organs

They generally develop a strong emotional attachment to the parent of the opposite sex

Curiosity about sex may make parents uncomfortable and induce negative responses

The caregiver should teach responsible sexual information

Before answering ,understand what the child is asking

The caregiver should teach the child “good touch” and “bad touch”

31Slide 31

Dysfunction of bowel and bladder control

- daytime control by 4

-day and night control for bladder and bowel

Tonsillitis

32Nursing Care for the Preschooler in a Health Care Facility

Nursing Care for the Preschooler in a Health Care Facility

32

Use play to act out anxieties and learn what to expect from the hospital situation

Do not use a rectal thermometer

Do not scold a child for reverting to bedwetting

Follow home routines as closely as possible

Carefully explain all procedures to the child

Provide play material
