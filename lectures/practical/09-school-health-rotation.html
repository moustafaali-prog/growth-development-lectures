ROTATION: SCHOOL HEALTH

Chapter 7 — School Health Rotation

Health services for the school-age child (6-12 years): the school nurse's roles, school entry

appraisal, screening programs (vision, hearing, scoliosis, dental, weight status), immunization

compliance, emergency care and first aid, in-school medication, and communicable-disease

control.

Age range: 6 – 12 years

7.1 Site Profile — The School Health Program

School nurses assume a major role in the school health program and can significantly affect the

lives of school-age children. Working collaboratively with the school and community, they

provide health supervision, health counseling, and health education.

RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE SCHOOL NURSE

Education and interventions for acute and chronic illness, safety, health promotion, nutrition,

mental health, dental disease, and sexually transmitted infections.

For some children, especially those in poverty, the school nurse may be the only contact with illness

prevention and health promotion.

For children with chronic conditions: leadership within the school health team, assessing health

status, identifying barriers to learning, and developing an individualized health care plan.

In school-based health centers, nurse practitioners provide primary care — treatment of acute

illness, vision/hearing screening, sports participation examinations, nutrition counseling, and

anticipatory guidance.

Unlicensed assistive personnel (UAP) may be part of the team — state-certified paraprofessionals

who assist a professional under supervision.

7.2 School Entry Health Screening

Health appraisal at school entry covers screening tests (vision, hearing), measurements (height,

weight), and medical, dental, and psychologic examinations.

Appraisal item

Content

History

Interval history (feeding, elimination, sleep, behavior, past illness, allergies,

medications), immunization record; family health and psychosocial history

Measurements

Height and weight on CDC charts (2-20 yr); BMI for weight status — school-age

obesity prevalence ~17%; blood pressure annually

Physical exam

Head-to-toe (eye acuity — Snellen letters for school-age; ear/hearing — pure-tone

audiometry; spine — scoliosis screening §7.3; skin; teeth; abdomen; extremities)

Immunization

review

Check the record against the printed schedule (see Ch. 4 §4.5); vaccines are required

in most states before school entry

Psychologic / social

Stress signals to explore: stomach pains/headache, sleep problems, bed-wetting,

regression; domestic-violence exposure risk awareness

7.3 Screening Programs

Screen

Method and referral

Vision

Distance acuity with Snellen letters (or numbers, tumbling E, HOTV) — referral: fewer than 4 of

6 correct on the 20-foot (6-m) line; alignment: corneal light reflex + cover test at near (33 cm)

and far (6 m)

Hearing

Pure-tone audiometry at 0.5, 1, 2, and 4 kHz; fails if tones cannot be heard at 20 dB HL;

tympanometry for middle-ear function

Scoliosis

With the child standing in underpants (bra for older girls) observe from behind — asymmetry

of shoulders and hips; with forward bend (back parallel to the floor) observe from the side for

rib-cage asymmetry/prominence; investigate a slight limp, crooked hemline, or sore back;

inspect the spine for tufts of hair, dimples, or discoloration. Scoliosis is especially important in

girls

Weight

status /

BMI

Plot height and weight on CDC charts; BMI for age; school-age obesity prevalence ~17%,

higher in rural/socioeconomically distressed families; the school nurse takes an active role in

nutrition

Dental

Brush 2-3×/day for 2-3 minutes with fluoridated toothpaste; floss at least once daily; soft

toothbrush changed every 3-4 months; dental examinations every 6 months; dental sealants

recommended for all school-age children

7.4 Immunization Compliance & Records

Before school entry: immunizations are required in most states before entry, part of communicable-

disease-control attendance policy.

At every contact: verify the record, check live-vaccine timing, and arrange catch-up (Ch. 4 §4.5).

4Page 4

Routine boosters: the booster ages for this age band follow the official printed schedule; the

adolescent items (MenACWY, Tdap, HPV, annual influenza, HepB/HepA, varicella) are covered in

Chapter 8.

7.5 Emergency Care & First Aid

Emergency care & safety: emergency treatment (first aid), notification of parents, and transportation

of the ill or injured child to home or hospital.

Chronic conditions: develop an individualized health care plan (IHP) — the nurse assesses health

status and identifies barriers to education; known allergy/asthma/seizure students follow their IHP

and facility protocol.

Sports: participation physical examinations at least every 2 years; the school nurse practitioner

performs them in school-based centers.

First-aid training: first-aid measures and child CPR are taught to parents; facility drills and kit

contents follow school/ministry policy.

Injury-prevention baseline: accidental injury remains the leading cause of death for children 1-14

years; motor-vehicle crashes are the most common cause of severe accidental injury/death for

school-age children.

7.6 Medication Administration at School

SAFETY ANCHORS AND POLICY LABELS

Medications must have child-safety caps; never leave any medication within a child's reach; "child-

resistant" is not "child-proof".

Children with chronic conditions receive care coordinated through the individualized health care

plan.

UAP assist a professional under supervision — who may administer, storage, dose verification,

parental permission, and documentation follow the school's written policy and local law.

7.7 Health Education & Communicable Disease Control

Health education: the school curriculum is a planned, sequential kindergarten-to-grade-12 program

addressing physical, mental, emotional, and social dimensions of health.

5Page 5

Communicable-disease control: detection and exclusion of affected children, with policies for

readmission and attendance.

Nutrition services: school meal programs furnish one third of the daily recommended allowance; the

average school lunch may exceed guidelines for saturated and total fat — healthful choices (fruits,

vegetables, low-fat milk) are reinforced.

Hand hygiene: routine handwashing before and after every meal, toileting, and child contact.

Head lice, absentee surveillance, and reporting: school policies for detection,

exclusion/readmission, and disease reporting follow local regulations.
